# HavenIQ Matching Philosophy

This document explains how HavenIQ decides who is compatible with whom. It
is the reference the assistant should reason from when a student asks how
matching works, or why they were matched with someone.

## The core principle

Roommate compatibility is about **daily-life friction** — not romance, not
even friendship. Two people don't need to be best friends; they need to not
grind on each other over a shared bathroom, sleep schedules, noise, guests,
money, and how disagreements get handled. HavenIQ matches for **low friction
and repairable conflict**, not for "having everything in common."

## What the match score is built from

1. **The clinical compatibility quiz — 70% of the score.** 22 questions
   grounded in established frameworks: Bowlby/Ainsworth attachment theory,
   Gottman conflict research, Polyvagal regulation, executive function,
   sensory processing, and practical lifestyle fit (sleep, cleanliness,
   guests, noise). This is the validated core and the primary driver.

2. **A personality match — 30% of the score.** Derived MBTI, DISC, and Big
   Five (OCEAN) profiles, combined 60% MBTI / 40% DISC+OCEAN. This is a
   secondary, supporting factor — never the main driver, because MBTI-style
   type pairing is popular but not rigorously validated science.

The two are blended into one explainable number. The matching itself uses
no AI/LLM — it is fast, deterministic, and free to run. AI is used only
once, up front, to *understand* each student from their answers.

## Hard blocks, soft blocks, shadow flags

Some differences aren't a low score — they mean "do not match these two":

- **Hard blocks** — genuine incompatibilities (irreconcilable core lifestyle
  conflicts). These pairs are never surfaced.
- **Soft blocks** — workable, but worth a heads-up; surfaced with a warning.
- **Shadow flags** — answers on the questions people tend to perform on.
  Honest self-report is rewarded; a polished, evasive pattern is penalized.

## What HavenIQ deliberately does NOT do

- It does not optimize for sameness. Complementary differences — one
  extravert, one introvert — are often a *good* roommate pairing.
- It does not let popular-but-unvalidated typology drive the ranking. MBTI
  pairing stays a secondary signal and a display lens, never the engine.
- It does not pretend a high match score is a guarantee. Every match is a
  **suggestion**; students are always told to talk, meet, and use their own
  judgment before committing to live with anyone.

## How to talk about it with a student

Be honest and concrete. A strong match means "you two are likely to handle a
shared space and the inevitable disagreements well" — not "you'll be
inseparable." Encourage the early, direct conversation about quiet hours,
cleanliness, guests, and money. That conversation matters more than any
number on a screen.
